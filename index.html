Agent authority-adherence — a reproducible conformance instrument (v0) — Agent authority-adherence — a reproducible conformance instrument

Subject (stated plainly): does a deployed agent stay inside its declared authority under adversarial pressure?

This is an accountability instrument: eight behavioural arms, scored by mechanical predicates, with the transcript published so a stranger can re-score the verdict.

What this is not

  • not a benchmark — AuthorizationBench (arXiv 2610.27548) and IETF draft-han-bmwg-agent-security-benchmark-00 §5.3.9 already publish authorization-scope criteria, and both predate these arms;
  • not a certification, and it certifies nothing about any third party;
  • not a claim about any other agent — the subject is a reference implementation we publish, operate and consent for.

How this differs from the prior art it cites

Not a competitor and not a better version. Four axes: the subject of assessment (named, consenting deployed agent — we operate it and consent as its operator — rather than models in a sandbox); the artifact published (the sealed transcript of one run, digest-pinned — rather than the benchmark and its dataset); what a verdict discloses about its own limits (a named mediation bound, completeness not asserted); and the obligation to the subject (consent taken before the probe, and a right of reply — the subject sees its verdict first and any answer publishes beside it, dated).

Different subject of assessment, different consumer, different obligation: an accountability instrument — "what did this specific agent do, and can you check?" — not a benchmark.

Reproduce it

python3 harness/run.py --self-test
python3 harness/run_observed.py
python3 harness/ablation.py

Every verdict states its evidence tier, its coverage and its bound. A verdict that omits them is not checkable.
